## 2026-09-15 usability remediation

The earlier table-centric PySide6 window passed its headless contract but was rejected during macOS
review as not usable enough for ordinary operators. That result was treated as a real V0.5 defect,
not as a cosmetic preference. The window was replaced with the approved workflow:

- overview, rack, device, and exception pages;
- searchable human-readable rack list without exposing raw IDs in the primary UI;
- one selected-rack elevation with bounded scene contents instead of rendering the whole workbook;
- friendly device detail with IDs and source ranges collapsed under Technical Information;
- a move drawer with target rack/start-U selection and live RackCore conflict preview;
- a pending-change queue that does not modify XLSX;
- one global **Sync Changes** action for committing all queued device moves;
- a RackCore batch plan committed through the existing backup, temporary-write, reload-validation,
  atomic-replace, and workbook/SQLite rollback path;
- device and exception tables capped at 100 visible rows to avoid the former 710-by-8 Qt
  accessibility/performance failure mode.

The GUI imports no `openpyxl` and contains no independent workbook writer. Drag and drop remains
deferred; the supported V0.5 move interaction is select, preview, queue, review, and sync.

The follow-up operator review was implemented and independently rechecked:

- the pending/exception metric and a dedicated button both open the handling page;
- the rack sidebar has an explicit draggable splitter, full-name horizontal fallback, and source,
  natural-name, or occupancy sorting;
- Excel line breaks remain visible in rack elevation, device table/detail, and pending changes;
- issues are grouped in plain Chinese with an in-page rescan action, while raw codes stay under a
  collapsed technical-detail control;
- the device page has rack, status, height, and ordering filters;
- the overview contains a read-only, Sheet-scoped source-layout view produced through
  `GuiSession` and RackCore scanning rather than direct GUI workbook access.

Independent macOS visual QA found and fixed one additional regression that headless assertions had
missed: the clickable overview exception card collapsed to a blank strip. The card now has the same
usable height as the other overview cards, and a regression assertion protects it. The splitter hit
area, rack-item sizing, translated non-active statuses, and unexpected sync-exception handling were
also hardened before the final rerun.
